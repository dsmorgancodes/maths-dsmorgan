This is the working archive of Drew M. Schaffner, M.S. Mathematics, Northwest Missouri State University. It collects notes, papers, computational projects, and proofs from graduate study, along with the code and graphics behind them.

A mission in mathematics

I have long been interested in Mathematics, I hold a masters degree in the discipline. But, my peers often wonder what it is exactly that I would like to do with the degree. The answer here is that I want to know more. I want to understand mathematics so as to understand the making of the universe. The handiwork of the heavens written in a language so vastly wide and large that we have yet to uncover even a fraction of it's conventions. Mathematics is the language that God spoke when he said "Let there be light." To know that language, is to know of what God has done. And I can think of no better thing to do with my time on earth, than to understand the workings of my Master.

So, is this a selfish endeavor? Yes, by all means. But need it necessarily be entirely so? I shall not think so. I hope that you can use this site to excel in your mathematical understanding. I know, for certain, that it has helped me.

Presuppositions

Standard proof techniques (direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction and induction) are assumed throughout. The Foundations notes cover the essentials, and Richard Hammack's Book of Proof, free online, covers them in full. Each set of notes states the results it relies on, and cites the textbook it follows by chapter. Some steps that seem obvious to me may not seem obvious to you. If so, the cited chapter is the place to look.

Code

Code on this site is written in Python. Projects pair a written account with the scripts that produce their figures and animations, so every graphic can be rebuilt from source. To run them you will need Python and the packages each project lists.

How to use this site

Notes are organised by course and typeset in LaTeX. Every theorem worth keeping also has its own short note, so a result can be read on its own or in the chapter where it is used.
